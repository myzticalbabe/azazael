# Admissibility pins — the demands a court makes of a machine's account

**Purpose.** This is the evidence side of `tools/esquire_admissibility.py`. The
instrument grades a proffer; this ledger says what the proffer has to *show*, where
that demand comes from, and what answering it still does not prove.

**Rule of the ledger: no assertion without its pin.** Every pin carries the operative
language that makes the demand, the tier at which that language was read, the artifact
that answers it, and the limit of what it establishes. A pin may not be quoted beyond
its tier. The canon article these pins support is E-11 in
`docs/ESQUIRE_CANON_2026-11-03.md`.

**Machine check.** `python tools/esquire_pins.py --gates` reads both this ledger's
`pins.json` and the instrument's own `GATE_METADATA`. It fails a gate that loses its
proof-tier pin, a pin that invents a gate, a tier-A pin whose text was never read, and
a declared gap that loses its pins.

| verdict | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `READY` | every implemented gate has a proof-tier pin; ledger has no defect |
| `INCOMPLETE` | no defect, but a gate's only support is a carried citation |
| `GAPS` | a gate has no proof-tier pin, or the ledger is malformed |
| `BLIND` | the ledger or the instrument cannot be read — not a pass |

## 1. The tiers, and why they exist

| tier | means | may be quoted for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | operative text read at source this session; URL and status recorded | the proposition in the pin, and no wider |
| **B** | confirmed at an official index or as recorded elsewhere; full text not read | the narrow fact confirmed, never the holding's reasoning |
| **C** | confirmed to exist; not read | *that it exists* only |
| **UNVERIFIABLE-HERE** | a probe was made and failed | nothing. It may not be a gate's sole support |

## 2. The demands

`→` names the artifact that answers the demand. `limit` names what it does not prove.

### D1 — Is there even a statement? (pin P1, tier A)

> "**Statement**" means a person's oral assertion, written assertion, or nonverbal
> conduct, if the person intended it as an assertion. "**Declarant**" means the
> person who made the statement.
> — FRE 801(a)–(b)

→ the output itself, plus the process record; there is no person to name.
**limit** — not relevance, not authenticity, not reliability, not weight. This pin
clears a threshold and answers nothing else. It is also the pin that inverts the
common error: a machine is *not* excluded because it is not a person; non-personhood
is what removes the hearsay bar.

### D2 — Is the item what it is claimed to be? (P2, P3, P4, P5 — tier A)

> To satisfy the requirement of authenticating or identifying an item of evidence, the
> proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what
> the proponent claims it is. — **FRE 901(a)**

> Evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate
> result. — **FRE 901(b)(9)**

> A record generated by an electronic process or system that produces an accurate
> result, as shown by a certification of a qualified person … The proponent must also
> meet the notice requirements of Rule 902(11). — **FRE 902(13)**

> An original writing, recording, or photograph is required in order to prove its
> content … For electronically stored information, "original" means any printout — or
> other output readable by sight — if it accurately reflects the information.
> — **FRE 1002; 1001(d)**

→ a written process description naming version, inputs, transformations and outputs;
a measured accurate result; or a qualified person's certification with pre-trial
notice; plus an output that accurately reflects the stored information.
**limit** — a hash answers *integrity*, not *accuracy of the process*. The ledger keeps
those apart instead of trading on the word "hash". 901(a)'s threshold is "sufficient to
support a finding", not proof.

### D3 — Was it kept in the ordinary course? (P6 — tier A)

> A record of an act, event, condition, opinion, or diagnosis if: (A) the record was
> made at or near the time by — or from information transmitted by — someone with
> knowledge; (B) the record was kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity
> …; (C) making the record was a regular practice of that activity; (D) all these
> conditions are shown by the testimony of the custodian or another qualified witness,
> or by a certification that complies with Rule 902(11) or (12) …; and (E) the opponent
> does not show that the source of information or the method or circumstances of
> preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness.
> — **FRE 803(6)**

→ the record with timestamp and originating event; evidence the keeping is a regular
practice and not a litigation artifact; a custodian or a 902(11) certification; an
anticipated answer to (E).
**limit** — (A)'s "someone with knowledge" is the weak joint for machine output and is
where an opponent will press. A self-timestamping system satisfies nothing by that fact.

### D4 — If it is an opinion, who is the witness? (P7 — tier A; P17 — tier A)

> A witness who is qualified as an expert by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or
> education may testify in the form of an opinion or otherwise if the proponent
> demonstrates to the court that it is more likely than not that: (a) … will help the
> trier of fact …; (b) the testimony is based on sufficient facts or data; (c) the
> testimony is the product of reliable principles and methods; and (d) the expert's
> opinion reflects a reliable application of the principles and methods to the facts.
> — **FRE 702** (as amended eff. 1 Dec. 2023)

→ a named natural person with stated qualifications; the facts or data relied on; a
versioned method with an evaluation; a showing of application to these facts.
**limit** — 702's subject is "a witness". No subdivision supplies a route by which a
model is sworn, qualified or cross-examined. The machine's account enters as data
(D2–D3) or through a human who adopts it; the two routes are not the same route.
*Daubert* (P17) is verified only as cited in FRE 702's Committee Note; the opinion was
not read, so the "non-exhaustive factors" characterisation is not quoted here.

### D5 — The criminal room (P8, P9 — tier A; P10 — tier B)

> Held: The State's use of Sylvia's statement violated the Confrontation Clause
> because, where testimonial statements are at issue, the only indicium of reliability
> sufficient to satisfy constitutional demands is confrontation. — ***Crawford***,
> 541 U.S. 36 (2004), Reporter's syllabus

> Under *Crawford*, a witness's testimony against a defendant is inadmissible unless
> the witness appears at trial or, if the witness is unavailable, the defendant had a
> prior opportunity for cross-examination … The certificates here are affidavits, which
> fall within the "core class of testimonial statements" covered by the Confrontation
> Clause. — ***Melendez-Diaz***, 557 U.S. 305 (2009), Reporter's syllabus

→ a characterisation of the statement as non-testimonial, with its basis stated; or the
declarant's availability and a prior opportunity to cross-examine.
**limit** — a syllabus "constitutes no part of the opinion" (the pages say so). These
are syllabus quotes, recorded as such. Whether a given certificate is testimonial is a
ruling on facts, not a reading of a rule. *Bullcoming* (P10) could not be fetched — the
LII paths returned HTTP 500 and 404 — so it remains tier B and may not be quoted.

### D6 — The room that will actually hear it first (P11 — tier A)

> Any oral or documentary evidence may be received, but the agency as a matter of policy
> shall provide for the exclusion of irrelevant, immaterial, or unduly repetitious
> evidence. A sanction may not be imposed or rule or order issued except on
> consideration of the whole record … and supported by and in accordance with the
> reliable, probative, and substantial evidence. … A party is entitled to … conduct such
> cross-examination as may be required for a full and true disclosure of the facts. …
> The transcript of testimony and exhibits, together with all papers and requests filed
> in the proceeding, constitutes the exclusive record for decision …
> — **5 U.S.C. § 556(d)–(e)**

→ the standard the forum applies, named in the record; disclosure of the output; a
recorded opportunity to respond and to cross-examine; a complete transcript and
exhibit set.
**limit** — that the tribunal followed its own rule, and the vacatur standard for an
award, are not modelled.

### D7 — Conditional relevance (P12 tier B; and see the declared gap)

Machine output is the textbook case of conditional relevance: the output means
something only if the input was what it claims to be. FRE 401's Committee Note routes
that question to Rules 104(b) and 901 — not to 401 alone.

## 4. The declared gap: G11 does not exist

**Measured, not supposed:**
`grep -E "\b(401|402|403|104)\b" tools/esquire_admissibility.py` → **zero matches**
(2026-11-04). The instrument has ten gates and not one of them asks whether the output
is relevant.

**Why that is a defect and not a gap in scope.** FRE 402: "Irrelevant evidence is not
admissible." A proffer can be authenticated (G5), reliable (G3), non-hearsay (G6) and
still excluded, because none of those gates names a fact of consequence. Every existing
gate *presupposes* a fact of consequence that none of them asks for.

**What this ledger does about it today:** opens P18 (401), P19 (402), P20 (403) as
tier-A pins against a gate that does not exist, and declares the gap in `pins.json`.
The checker fails the ledger if a declared gap loses its pins — so the gap cannot
quietly close or quietly widen.

**What it does not do:** implement G11. That is a change to the instrument and its 71
tests, with its own falsifier and negative control, and it is deliberately not smuggled
in here. The specification is written and pinned; the gate is next.

**Why not extend the vocabulary further.** Each gate costs a pin (a text read at
source), a falsifier, a negative control and tests. Gates outside the federal rules —
state evidence codes, the FAA, unauthorised-practice rules — have no single text to
pin, so an extended vocabulary would manufacture exactly the assertion class this
ledger forbids. And the instrument's own verdict words must **not** become
"admissible"/"inadmissible": it grades a proffer, and its header refuses that word
because an instrument that could output "inadmissible" would be doing a court's job
badly. One gate, at the front of the chain, is the correct extension.

## 6. What this ledger is not

- **Not legal advice**, and not a prediction of any ruling. It records what rules
  require and what evidence answers them.
- **Not a substitute for the forum's own rules.** State evidence codes, arbitration
  rules and agency regulations differ; § 556 says the agency applies its own.
- **Not exhaustive of admissibility.** Privilege, the original-writings exceptions,
  and every exclusionary rule outside 401–403 and 801–807 are untouched.
- **Not a claim that a machine is a witness.** E-11.5 states the opposite, and P7's text
  is the reason.
