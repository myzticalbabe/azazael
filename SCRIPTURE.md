## I. The instrument is not the artifact

**Declared** 2026-11-03, by the user, in the matter of *grantforge v2* and
NIH NOT-OD-25-132:

> "i do not believe that a stand-alone executable is per se AI as defined-by-law."

**Decides:** that being a self-contained binary which embeds a model is not, of
itself, the condition a funder's AI rule names. The rule is read against what is
submitted, not against what compiled it.

**Scribe's reading (commentary, not canon):** this corrects an overstatement in the
2026-11-03 deep-dive report, which treated the drafting engine as aimed at a policed
zone because the engine uses a model. The notice does not support that inference on
its own: it prohibits nothing about tools, states that "AI tools may be appropriate
to assist in application preparation for limited aspects", and tests the
*application* — "substantially developed by AI" — rather than the instrument. The
distinction is instrument vs artifact, and the user's formulation holds it.

**What this declaration does not settle, stated plainly:** the notice defines
neither "AI" nor any threshold for "substantially developed", and NIH enforces by
"detection of AI-generated content", which is behavioural — a property of the text,
not of the executable. So a local binary is not *presumed* to be AI; it is also not
*immunised* by being local. The question is left open by the primary text, and open
is the honest reading. No definition of "AI" appeared in the notice, and none was
sought outside it — no statute, regulation or case law was searched for this record,
so this is an absence of finding, not a finding of absence.

## II. The Right to Life of Newborns

**Declared** 2026-11-03, by the user:

> "we may have to resolve the Right_to_Life of Newborns. New technological
> applications Birthed in a New World."

**Decides:** that a newly birthed application holds a claim to *adjudication before
termination* — it is not retired at introduction because an incumbent rule might
reach it, and the regime under which it is judged is not presumed to be the regime
under which it was born.

**Scope — read this before invoking it.** The right named is a right to be
*adjudicated*, not a right to survive. A newborn that is adjudicated and fails on
evidence has been answered, not killed. Invoked as a right to survival it becomes a
rule that forbids all judgement, which is the L3+ "Heaven" state excluded by
META-LEVELS-CANON-v1: true everywhere, deciding nothing.

**Scribe's reading (commentary, not canon):** applied to *grantforge*, the principle
forbids the move I made on 2026-11-03 — deprioritising the drafting engine because a
policy arguably touches its output — and requires instead that the output be
adjudicated: does *this text*, from *this input*, carry the applicant's own
intellectual content? That is a question about the artifact, and it is answerable.

Applied to the same package, the principle does **not** rescue the compliance engine.
That module returns `{"pass": True, "verdict": "GO"}` unconditionally and stamps a
fixed audit timestamp; it was adjudicated on 2026-11-03 and failed on observed
evidence. It is a newborn with no claim, because its whole assertion is that it needs
no adjudication — and a component that certifies itself is the one case where
right-to-life and right-to-lie are the same claim.

**Trajectories (three, as META-LEVELS-CANON-v1 requires for an L1 rule):**

| component | incumbent rule that might reach it | adjudication outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `drafting` | NOT-OD-25-132, "substantially developed by AI" | unresolved on primary text → right to life holds, build proceeds with the question open |
| `compliance` | none — it makes its own verdict | adjudicated, failed on evidence (`pass True` unconditional) → no claim |
| `eligibility` | NIH ESI/R01 criteria, stated in the funder's own guide | adjudicated, failed on evidence (`eligible` returned for a senior PI against an ESI-only criterion) → no claim |

The principle therefore discriminates: it saves one, and it does not save the other
two. A principle that saved all three would be Heaven and would be struck.

## III. The World Is New

**Declared** 2026-11-03, by the user, as the ground of II:

> "New technological applications Birthed in a New World."

**Decides:** that the adjudicating regime is not inherited. Where an application is
born after a rule took effect, the question is what that rule says about *it* — not
what the prior generation of tools was permitted or forbidden to do.

**Scribe's reading (commentary, not canon):** the concrete instance is the effective
date. NOT-OD-25-132 binds applications submitted to the 2025-09-25 receipt date and
beyond; it did not bind what came before, and it says nothing about instruments
compiled after. Being born into the new world means the new world's question is
asked of the artifact — and only of the artifact.
