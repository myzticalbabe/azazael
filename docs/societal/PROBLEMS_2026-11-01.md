# Six social problems this tree could answer by sharing pieces (2026-11-01)

Recorded at the user's instruction: web-research problems of society that this codebase
could address as **open-source projects built by sharing pieces**, and record the findings as
goals and tasks. The machine-readable record is [`tasks.json`](tasks.json) — **Track F,
`S-01` … `S-06`**. This page is the evidence copy: every number below carries its date and a
source tier, because a problem statement without a source is an opinion.

**Source tiers** (FLOCK canon): `A` primary document · `B` official index / institutional
publication · `C` credible secondary · `D` snippet or social post. Nothing here is used at
tier `D` on its own.

## Method and its limit

Six searches, run 2026-11-01, on: the digital divide and device reuse; the civil justice gap;
AI companions and child safety; public-records backlogs; nonprofit security; e-waste. What
that yields is **six problems with real, dated numbers and a plausible open-source answer**.
It does not yield evidence that any of these projects would be adopted, funded or used — and
adoption, not code, is what these problems are short of. That gap is stated per project under
*Honest limit*.

## 1. The device divide is a software problem as much as a hardware one

- **21 million US students lack home internet access** (2026-04-09, tier D — a vendor blog
  repeating a widely used figure; treat as an order of magnitude, not a measurement).
- **62 Mt of e-waste generated in 2022, 22.3% properly recycled** (2025-02-26, tier C,
  citing the Global E-waste Monitor).
- Schools retire **expired Chromebooks** while the same families lack a working device
  (2023 thread, tier D — indicative of the pattern, not of a rate).

The reusable insight: the hardware exists and the software is what expires. A shard that
runs in a browser tab or a 2019 laptop is worth more than a donation drive.

## 2. The civil justice gap is a paperwork gap

- **92% of low-income Americans received no or insufficient legal help for their civil legal
  problems** (LSC, Justice Gap report, tier B).
- Working tools already exist and are *form assembly*, not advice: Massachusetts' **MADE**
  prepares the seven forms a self-represented tenant needs (2025-10-15, tier B), and LA's
  **Tenant Power Toolkit** fills eviction responses (tier B).

The pattern to copy: deterministic document assembly with citations, not a chatbot that
practises law.

## 3. AI companions arrived before anyone built the safety substrate

- **California signed 13 bills on youth-facing AI and online services on 2026-09-10**,
  including companion-chatbot safeguards and limits on K-12 pupil data in AI systems
  (tier A, Governor's press release).
- **New York required a self-harm detection protocol from 2025-11-05** (2025-10-30, tier C).
- UNICEF published a six-jurisdiction policy brief on AI companions and children's rights
  (2026, tier A); the European Parliament published its own briefing (2026, tier A).

Every operator now needs: a crisis protocol, a disclosure, age assurance, and a local record
of what the model said. That is *infrastructure*, and small operators cannot buy it.

## 4. Public records are backing up faster than anyone can process them

- **≈340,000 overdue FOIA requests** at federal agencies as of 2025-09-30 (NYT, 2026-07-30,
  tier C); GAO has documented the backlog pattern for years (tier A/B).

The bottleneck is triage, redaction and appeal-letter bookkeeping — volunteer-shaped work,
not lawyer-shaped work.

## 5. Small organisations are the ransomware economy's main customer

- **Over 2 in 5 charities report limited or no preparation** for a cyber-attack, and **32%
  faced an attack** in a 2026 survey (tier D, insurer post — direction only).
- **17 registered nonprofit ransomware victims in January 2026 alone** (2026-06-26, tier C).
- A widely repeated vendor claim that 96% of 2026 ransomware victims are small organisations
  is tier D and is **not** used as a premise here.

What a small org lacks is not a scanner; it is the **evidence pack** an insurer or regulator
asks for after the fact.

## 6. Attribution is now a creator problem, not a platform problem

AI-generated material competes with human work, and the only defence a creator has is a
**provable lineage**: this file, this author, this history. Content-addressing plus a
provenance chain is that defence, and it needs no platform account.

## The six grafts (what "sharing pieces" means concretely)

The shared piece is the **Shard** contract already built in this tree: content-addressed
identity, an exact integer simulation, a declared presentation tier ladder, a provenance
chain, and a checker that refuses what it cannot verify. Grafting it onto a social problem
means shipping the piece, not a platform: no accounts, no server, no telemetry.

### S-01 · Second-life device kit — *problem 1*

- **Answer:** a runner plus three offline learning/utility shards that declare a hard
  low-end tier ladder, each under the existing low-end ceiling.
- **Pieces:** `docs/METTHEW/highend_families.json` (`ladder`), the low-end caps in
  `tools/metthew_validate.py` (**2 MiB / 24 files**), `docs/METTHEW/reference-shards/`.
- **First artefact:** three `.mett` shards + one `run-anywhere` page, with a cold-start
  measurement on the weakest machine available.
- **Honest limit:** the hardware is the donor's, the support is the school's; we can only
  make the software not expire.

### S-02 · Tenant answer packet — *problem 2*

- **Answer:** a deterministic form-assembly shard: offline questionnaire → a packet of the
  local forms with the answers filled and the filing deadlines computed from a dated table.
  It produces a **packet**, never advice, and prints a hash of what it produced so a legal
  aid worker can verify the exact document.
- **Pieces:** the shard contract (golden run + headless proof), `provenance`/
  `author_pubkey` in `docs/METTHEW/shard.schema.json`, and the refusal discipline in
  `tools/metthew_validate.py`.
- **First artefact:** one county's forms, 12 questions, deadline table with a
  `rules_version` date.
- **Honest limit:** the law changes; an un-updated rules table is worse than no tool, so the
  gate must refuse a stale `rules_version` rather than answer.

### S-03 · Companion safety kernel — *problem 3*

- **Answer:** the open-source substrate a small operator can adopt: an on-device persona card
  with an **enumerated intent set** (the model proposes, the integer sim disposes), a crisis
  protocol with a hard-coded escalation path, local-only logs, and a compliance evidence pack
  mapping each control to the bill that asks for it.
- **Pieces:** the `persona` family (`intent-enum`, `persona-card`) in
  `docs/METTHEW/highend_families.json`; `src/PSYCHE/` (`affect.py`, `rt_agent.py`,
  `constructions.py`) for bounded affect modelling; the hash-chained ledger in `.audit/`
  (`anchors.jsonl`) for "what did it say, when".
- **First artefact:** a persona card + crisis protocol + a test that the model cannot emit an
  intent outside the enum.
- **Honest limit:** a crisis protocol is a clinical and legal artefact; shipping one without
  a named human escalation partner is worse than shipping nothing, and the evidence pack is
  not legal advice.

### S-04 · Records backlog bench — *problem 4*

- **Answer:** a volunteer workbench for one agency's response set: ingest, deterministic
  triage, a redaction review queue, an appeal-letter skeleton with statutory dates, and a
  hash-chained log of every action so the work is defensible.
- **Pieces:** `.audit/` anchors + `src/CORE/compliance/`; the document-forensics discipline
  already practised in `.bcc_audit/03_tooling/` (`behavior_trace.py`, `process_corpus.py`);
  `tools/evidence_gate.py`.
- **First artefact:** a seeded 200-page response set, measured triage time, and a
  redaction recall/precision number with its negative control (a clean page must not be
  redacted).
- **Honest limit:** redaction errors are irreversible disclosures; the tool must be
  review-first, and any automation claim needs a measured false-negative rate.

### S-05 · Small-org evidence pack — *problem 5*

- **Answer:** one command a nonprofit runs quarterly that produces the artefact an insurer or
  regulator asks for: secret scan, dependency scan, backup integrity check, access review
  checklist, each with a hash-chained receipt.
- **Pieces:** the gate fleet in `tools/` (`staged_scanner_gate.py`,
  `compare_scans.py`, `evidence_gate.py`, `failure_evidence_gate.py`), `gitleaks`/`trivy`/
  `semgrep` already in the estate's tooling, and `.audit/` anchors.
- **First artefact:** one `pack.sh` + a report template + a "what we could not check" section
  that is never empty.
- **Honest limit:** an evidence pack records the state of a machine, not the absence of an
  attacker.

### S-06 · Provenance stamp for creators — *problem 6*

- **Answer:** stamp a file with a content-addressed id, an author key, and a lineage record
  (what it was made from, with hashes), and give the creator a one-page verifier anyone can
  run offline. No account, no platform.
- **Pieces:** `author_pubkey` + `provenance` in `docs/METTHEW/shard.schema.json`; the
  licence gate recorded as `M-15` in
  [`../METTHEW/tasks.json`](../METTHEW/tasks.json) (dissection rights); the same
  content-addressed id discipline as `bundle_fingerprint` in `tools/metthew_validate.py`.
- **First artefact:** `stamp <file> --author <key>` + `verify <file>` that works with no
  network.
- **Honest limit:** a stamp proves lineage, not authorship of an idea; it cannot stop a
  plagiarist, only deny them a clean claim.

## What this is not

- Not a platform, not a service, not a startup plan. Each graft is a **piece** someone can
  run offline, which is the only distribution model this tree already supports
  (`docs/METTHEW/REJECTION_ANALYSIS_2026-10-31.md` §R1).
- Not legal, medical or security advice. S-02, S-03 and S-04 each name the professional who
  must own the last mile.
- Not measured. **None of these six exist.** They are recorded tasks with acceptance
  criteria — S-01 is the only one whose build is a variation on something already running
  (`reference-shards/highend_ladder`).
