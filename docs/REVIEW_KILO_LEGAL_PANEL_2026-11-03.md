## 2. Six Panel Questions — Verdicts

### Question 1: Is `featherbedding` the right word, or is the analogy affirmatively misleading?

**VERDICT: MISLEADING — the memo itself concedes this at §0 lines 39-42.**

The memo correctly states: "§8(b)(6) binds *labour organisations*; no union appears in the AI rules. The honest form is: the AI-restriction pattern is **featherbedding-shaped**, and the statutory term does not reach it." (§0, ¶2)

**Better terms of art the memo missed:**

| Domain | Term | Why It Fits Better |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| Antitrust | **Professional restraint of trade** | *Goldfarb* + *Dental Examiners* reach guild-imposed substitution rules directly — no union required. |
| Licensing law | **Occupational licensing abuse** | State boards dominated by practitioners adopting rules that suppress cheaper substitutes. |
| EU free movement | **Disproportionate qualification requirement** | EU Professional Qualifications Directive (2005/36/EC) Art. 9: restrictions must be proportionate; AI-substitution rules may fail. |
| Administrative law | **Regulatory capture / rent-seeking** | *Dental Examiners*: when market participants control the regulator, the rule is presumptively self-dealing. |

**Panel consensus (Labour, Antitrust, EU/Data-Protection):** "Featherbedding" is a category error when applied to regulator-imposed requirements. The term carries statutory baggage (union exaction) that does not map. The memo should lead with *rent-seeking* or *professional restraint* and relegate "featherbedding" to an analogy with a disclaimer.

---

### Question 2: Are the five mechanisms actually one mechanism?

**VERDICT: NO — the fifth mechanism (funder originality) is a different species.**

The memo itself recognizes this at §1.5 (lines 153-167): "In mechanisms 1–4 the inserted human is installed *for the protection of a person* — the author, the inventor, the client, the injured party. In funder originality the human is installed for the protection of **the funder's own evaluation capacity**."

| Mechanism | Who Is Protected | Value Transferred To | Nature |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|--------|
| 1. Authorship | The author | The human who adopted the work | Rights instrument |
| 2. Inventorship | The inventor | The human named on patent | Rights instrument |
| 3. Professional responsibility | The client | The licensed professional | Rights instrument |
| 4. Liability | The injured party | The human provider/deployer | Rights instrument |
| 5. Funder originality | **The funder** | **The funder** (preserved evaluation capacity) | **Market-access condition** |

**Panel consensus (IP, Professional-Regulation, Adversarial):** Mechanisms 1-4 converge on a structural pattern: a rights-bearing natural person must stand at the point of origination. Mechanism 5 breaks the pattern — the applicant is *screened*, not protected. The memo should say: "Four mechanisms are one; the fifth is a convergent but distinct species."

---

### Question 3: Does the substitution axis survive?

**VERDICT: SUBSTANTIALLY YES — with one material qualification.**

**Strongest counter-example the memo did not name:** **NYC Local Law 144 (bias audits for AEDTs in employment).** This applies to *screening* tools that do not replace a human screener — they *augment* the process. The AI reads resumes; the human still decides. Yet LL 144 bites identically.

**Qualification:** The axis holds for *generative* substitution (AI drafts → human signs). It strains for *evaluative* AI (AI screens → human reviews). The memo's intra-document proof (NLnet policy: read-only permitted, write banned) is clean for prose; the artefact clause ("AI-generated artefacts barred") is a second axis — a generation ban, not a substitution rule.

**Panel consensus (Antitrust, Professional-Regulation, Adversarial):** The substitution axis is the strongest structural claim in the memo. The counter-example is real but narrow: evaluative AI in employment is the only regime biting non-substituting use as hard as substituting use. The memo should acknowledge this boundary explicitly.

---

### Question 4: Is the antitrust lever live or academic?

**VERDICT: LIVE — but with standing and Parker-supervision hurdles.**

| Hurdle | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Active market participants?** | YES — bar associations, medical boards, state licensing boards are practitioner-dominated. *Dental Examiners* applies directly. |
| **Active supervision absent?** | YES — most state boards lack meaningful executive/legislative review of anticompetitive rules. |
| **Restraint of trade?** | YES — *Goldfarb*: "nature of an occupation does not provide sanctuary from the Sherman Act." Substitution rules suppress cheaper competitors. |
| **Antitrust injury / standing?** | **MAJOR GAP** — No AI-context antitrust case exists. A plaintiff not yet in the market (e.g., an AI grant-tool vendor) must show *antitrust injury* (injury of the type the antitrust laws prevent). *Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat*, 429 U.S. 477 (1977). A vendor excluded by a substitution rule has injury-in-fact; but a plaintiff "not yet in the market" faces *Atlantic Richfield v. USA Petroleum*, 495 U.S. 328 (1990) barriers. |
| **Parker active supervision — what does it look like?** | *Dental Examiners*: supervisor must review substance, have veto power, not be a market participant. Most state AG offices lack bandwidth for this. |

**Panel consensus (Antitrust, Adversarial):** The lever is *structurally live* — the doctrine maps cleanly. The first case will likely be an AI tool vendor excluded by a state bar rule on "unauthorized practice of law" or a medical board rule on "AI diagnosis." Standing is the real fight. The memo should add a standing analysis.

### Question 5: Is the humanwashing/GDPR Art. 22 reading correct?

**VERDICT: SUBSTANTIALLY CORRECT — with one over-read.**

**Art. 22 GDPR text:** "The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based **solely on automated processing**... which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her."

**Wilson et al. reading (confirmed):** Insert a nominal human → decision is no longer "solely automated" → Art. 22 protections (right to human review, meaningful information, contest) do not apply.

**Over-read in memo:** "The paper's reading of the AI Liability Directive — since withdrawn — is the same shape: systems in which a human played a role were excluded, which created 'an incentive to notionally describe a system as having a human in the loop without being any more explicit.'" (§2, lines 189-192)

**Correction:** The AI Liability Directive (COM(2022) 496) was *withdrawn* in 2024. A withdrawn instrument proves nothing about current law. The memo uses it to show *legislative intent pattern*, not current law — but this distinction is blurred. The withdrawal was political (lack of consensus), not a repudiation of the human-in-the-loop carve-out logic.

**EDPB Guidance (2022, 2023):** Confirms that "meaningful human review" requires the human to have *authority and competence to change the outcome* — not merely rubber-stamp. A nominal human fails this test.

**Panel consensus (EU/Data-Protection, Adversarial):** The core Art. 22 reading is correct and important. The withdrawn Directive reference should be marked "illustrative of legislative pattern, not evidence of current law."

| **NYC LL 144** | In force | Bias audits for AEDTs — *not deferred* |
| **EU AI Act Art. 14** | In force | Human oversight obligation — *kept* |
| **Illinois BIPA** | In force | Biometric consent — reaches AI training data |
| **Canada C-27 (AIDA)** | Royal Assent 2024 | Federal AI regulator — *not US but same pattern* |

**Missing relaxations (in the other direction):**
| Instrument | Status |
|------------|--------|

**Panel consensus (Adversarial, Professional-Regulation):** The memo's §3 is structurally honest (it admits the premise is half-wrong) but the *catalogue* is incomplete. The six omitted tightening regimes are material — they are the ones actually biting in 2026. The memo should either list them or explicitly scope the counter-evidence section.
