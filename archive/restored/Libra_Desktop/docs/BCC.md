## Introduction

The acronym **"BCC"** in the Libra project context refers to the **Berks County Court of Common Pleas** (23rd Judicial District, Reading, PA). However, a separate entity also known as **"BCC"** — the **Berks Counseling Center** — operates in the same city and shares a deep collaborative relationship with the court system. This document provides a comprehensive profile of the Berks Counseling Center and explains the intersection between the two BCC entities.

## Part 2: Non-Profit Status & Tax-Exempt Status

### 501(c)(3) Designation

Berks Counseling Center is classified as a **501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization** under the Internal Revenue Code. This means:

- **Tax-Deductible Donations:** Contributions made to BCC are tax-deductible to the fullest extent of the law.
- **Public Charity Status:** BCC operates as a public charity (not a private foundation), meaning it receives broad public support rather than funding from a single source.
- **IRS Filing Requirements:** BCC files annual IRS Form 990 returns, which are publicly available through the IRS and ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer.

### Pennsylvania Non-Profit Status

BCC is registered as a non-profit corporation in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and is in good standing with the Pennsylvania Department of State, Bureau of Corporations and Charitable Organizations. As a non-profit providing direct services, BCC is exempt from Pennsylvania state income tax under 72 P.S. § 7201. Additionally, BCC may be exempt from local property taxes due to its charitable purpose, though specific property tax exemption status depends on the nature of its real estate holdings and their use.

### Key Non-Profit Governance Requirements

- **Board of Directors:** Governed by a volunteer board of directors with fiduciary responsibility.
- **Executive Sessions:** Regular public filings of board composition and executive compensation.
- **Independent Audit:** Subject to annual independent CPA audit due to federal grant expenditures exceeding $750,000.

## Part 3: Public Funding Sources — Deep Dive

### Revenue Overview

BCC operates on an annual budget exceeding **$12.5 million**. The vast majority of revenue (~92-95%) comes from **Program Services Revenue**, which includes:

### 1. Federal Funding

- **Medicaid (Medical Assistance):** The single largest revenue source. BCC is enrolled as a Pennsylvania Medicaid provider and bills for psychiatric, counseling, substance abuse, and case management services.
- **SAMHSA Grants:** BCC has received federal Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) grants, including the **CCBHC Expansion Grant** (Grant #H79SM088878, awarded in 2023) to support and expand its Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic model.
- **Medicare:** BCC accepts Medicare Part B for eligible beneficiaries requiring behavioral health services.

### 2. State Funding (Pennsylvania)

- **PA Department of Human Services (DHS):** BCC operates under contracts from PA DHS, which administers behavioral health funding through county-based systems. DHS oversees:
  - The **Medical Assistance (MA) Fee-for-Service** program
  - **HealthChoices** (PA's managed care Medicaid program)
  - **Community Behavioral Health (CBH)** — the Berks County behavioral health managed care organization
- **PA Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs (DDAP):** Funding for substance use disorder treatment services.
- **Human Services Block Grant:** Funding allocated through the county under PA's Human Services Block Grant program, allowing flexible use of state funds across multiple service categories.

### 3. County Funding (Berks County)

- **Berks County Mental Health/Developmental Disabilities (MH/DD):** BCC holds contracts to provide mental health services to county-eligible residents, including individuals who are uninsured or underinsured.
- **Council on Chemical Abuse (COCA):** The Berks County drug and alcohol authority contracts with BCC for substance abuse treatment services.
- **Berks County Human Services Plan:** BCC is listed as a key vendor/provider in the county's annual Human Services Plan submitted to PA DHS.

### 4. Insurance Contracts

BCC accepts and bills a wide range of insurance plans:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Government** | Pennsylvania Medical Assistance (Medicaid), Medicare Part B, CHIP |
| **Commercial** | Highmark Blue Shield, UPMC Health Plan, Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare |
| **Managed Care** | Community Behavioral Health (CBH), PA HealthChoices plans |
| **Military** | TRICARE, Veterans Affairs (VA) community care |

### 5. United Way of Berks County

BCC has been a beneficiary of **United Way of Berks County** focused grants, including:
- **MacKenzie Scott Impact Grants:** Used to expand Intensive Outpatient Program (IOP) capacity.
- **Mental Health Support Initiatives:** Ongoing programmatic funding for family-centered health and wellness.
- **Community Impact Fund:** General operating support through United Way's annual campaign.

### 6. Private Donations & Fundraising

As a 501(c)(3), BCC accepts private donations from individuals, corporations, and foundations. Donations are tax-deductible and can be directed to specific programs or the general operating fund.

### CCBHC Designation

Since 2017, BCC has operated as a **Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic (CCBHC)** — a prestigious federal designation through SAMHSA.

**What CCBHC Means:**
- **No-Wrong-Door Model:** Clients receive comprehensive care regardless of diagnosis, insurance status, or ability to pay.
- **Integrated Care:** Mental health, substance use, and primary care screening are coordinated under one roof.
- **Cost-Based Reimbursement:** CCBHCs are reimbursed at a prospective payment system (PPS) rate, which provides financial stability.
- **9 Core Services:** Crisis services, screening/assessment, patient-centered treatment planning, outpatient care, case management, peer support, psychiatric services, primary care screening, and military/veteran services.

**PA Context:**
- BCC was one of only **seven CCBHCs in Pennsylvania** at the time of its original designation.
- The program is overseen by **PA Department of Human Services (DHS)** in partnership with SAMHSA.
- In 2018, state and federal officials toured BCC to highlight the program's success.

## Part 7: Relationship Between the Two BCC Entities

### Structural Independence

**Berks Counseling Center (BCC)** and the **Berks County Court of Common Pleas (BCC)** are **separate, legally independent entities** with no corporate, ownership, or governance relationship:

| Aspect | Berks Counseling Center | Berks County Court |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Type** | Private 501(c)(3) non-profit | Government judicial branch |
| **Governance** | Board of Directors | PA Supreme Court / President Judge |
| **Funding** | Medicaid, grants, insurance, donations | County/state government appropriations |
| **Mission** | Behavioral health treatment | Adjudication of legal matters |

### Collaborative Relationship — Treatment Courts

Despite being independent entities, BCC and the Berks County Court have a deep **operational and contractual partnership** through the county's **Treatment Court programs**:

#### Mental Health Treatment Court
- Participants with mental health diagnoses are diverted from incarceration into court-supervised treatment.
- **BCC's Role:** Primary clinical provider — conducts assessments, provides therapy and case management, reports compliance to the court.
- **Court's Role:** Judicial oversight — monitors progress, sanctions non-compliance, incentivizes recovery.

#### Drug Treatment Court
- Alternative sentencing for non-violent offenders with substance use disorders.
- **BCC's Role:** Substance abuse treatment provider — intensive outpatient, drug testing, counseling.
- **Court's Role:** Regular status hearings, progress reviews, graduation determinations.

#### How the Partnership Works

```
Berks County Court                        Berks Counseling Center
(23rd Judicial District)                   (Private Non-Profit)
         |                                         |
         | 1. Court refers defendant                |
         |    to BCC for assessment  ---------->    |
         |                                         |
         | 2. BCC evaluates and                    |
         |    recommends tx plan     <----------    |
         |                                         |
         | 3. Court orders treatment                |
         |    as condition of sentence  -------->   |
         |                                         |
         | 4. BCC provides treatment                |
         |    (counseling, case mgmt,               |
         |     drug testing, etc.)                  |
         |                                         |
         | 5. BCC reports compliance                |
         |    and progress           <----------    |
         |                                         |
         | 6. Court adjusts supervision             |
         |    based on clinical input               |
```

#### Contractual Basis
- BCC holds **service contracts** with Berks County (via MH/DD, COCA) to provide treatment to court-involved individuals.
- These contracts are renewed annually as part of the **Berks County Human Services Plan**.
- BCC is listed by name in official court participant handbooks as a designated treatment provider.

### Summary

**No legal or corporate connection exists between the two BCCs.** However, they maintain an extensive **professional service relationship** in which:

1. The **Berks County Court** acts as the referring and oversight body
2. **Berks Counseling Center** acts as the clinical treatment provider
3. **Berks County** acts as the intermediary funder and contract administrator

This treatment court partnership represents a sophisticated collaboration between the judicial and behavioral health systems — part of a national movement toward therapeutic jurisprudence and alternatives to incarceration.

### 10C — Recommended Filing Sequence

For most complaints, follow this order:

1. **Internal grievance** — File directly with Berks Counseling Center first (most funders require exhaustion of internal grievance)
2. **Primary regulator** — Based on issue type per routing matrix above
3. **Secondary regulator** — If primary doesn't resolve within 60 days, escalate
4. **Legal counsel** — For litigation or qui tam (whistleblower) actions, consult an attorney
5. **Legislative** — Contact PA State Representative or Senator for systemic issues

#### Exhaustion Requirements

| Issue Type | Internal Grievance Required? | Timeline |
|------------|---------------------------|----------|
| Medicaid quality of care | Yes — BCC grievance process first | 30 days for BCC response |
| CCBHC standards | No — direct to SAMHSA | No exhaustion required |
| HIPAA violation | No — direct to HHS OCR | 180 days from discovery |
| Insurance denial | Yes — internal appeal first | Varies by plan |
| Employment discrimination | Yes — EEOC charge first | 180 days (PA) / 300 days (federal) |
| Consumer protection | Recommended — written demand first | 30 days (PA law) |
